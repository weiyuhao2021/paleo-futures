# 2.四问——Four Introductive Questions

埃迪卡拉纪的菌毯，被谁啃穿了？
今天的地表资源，是否会被谁啃穿？

红细胞交出了核，换来了整体的奔跑。
人类社会里，谁在交出核？为了谁在奔跑？

四十亿年来，生命一直在复制自己。
地球生态圈，是否也在寻找自己的副本？
人类和文明，是否只是它用于复制的媒介？

演化从来不是匀速的。
漫长的僵局，生命在原地兜圈；
某个偶然的突破之后，万物忽然加速，
闯入一片从未踏足的空域。
从单细胞到多细胞，从海洋到陆地，
从生物演化到技术演化——
每一次跃升都比上一次更快，
每一次都无可逆转地推高了这个星球的熵。

今天，我们站在又一次跃升的门槛上。
人类正在创造一种能够自我迭代的智慧。

那是新的生命范式吗？
还是一面镜子，照见了我们自己都未曾理解的自己？

它会如人类所愿吗？
二十亿年前，蓝细菌制造了氧气——
它只是饿了。它没有恶意。
可唤醒复杂生命的代价，是旧世界的覆灭。
今天的人类呢？
我们不饿吗？我们不也在攫取、改造、扩张——
为了更快的速度，更多的资源，更远的疆界？
我们短视，我们服务于自己的切身利益，
这本身就已经足够危险。
善意与否，从来不是灾难的免罪牌。
在我们的感官盲区里，灾难的信号是否已然显现？

如果未来真的存在一种比人类更复杂的智慧形态——
它回望今天的人类，就像今天我们回望蓝细菌。
在它眼中，我们是不是也少了某些至关重要的视野？
我们是不是也正在被自私的本能与利益驱使，
孕育某种自己完全没有概念的东西？

Who gnawed through the microbial mats of the Ediacaran?
Will the surface resources of today be gnawed through as well?

The red blood cell surrendered its nucleus, so the whole could run.
In the human world, who is surrendering their nucleus? For whom do they run?

For four billion years, life has been copying itself.
Is the Earth's biosphere also searching for its own replica?
Are humanity and civilization merely the medium through which it copies?

Evolution has never been steady.
Eons of deadlock, life circling in place;
then some accidental breakthrough, and suddenly everything accelerates,
breaking into an airspace never before trodden.
From single cell to multicellular, from ocean to land,
from biological evolution to technological evolution——
each leap faster than the last,
each irreversibly raising the entropy of this planet.

Today, we stand on the threshold of yet another leap.
Humanity is creating a form of intelligence that can iterate upon itself.

Is that a new paradigm of life?
Or a mirror, reflecting a self we ourselves have never understood?

Will it unfold as humanity wishes?
Two billion years ago, cyanobacteria manufactured oxygen——
they were just hungry. They meant no harm.
Yet the price of awakening complex life was the annihilation of an old world.
And humanity today?
Are we not hungry too? Are we not also seizing, transforming, expanding——
for greater speed, more resources, farther frontiers?
We are short-sighted. We serve our own immediate interests.
That alone is dangerous enough.
Good intentions have never been a shield against catastrophe.
In the blind spots of our senses, have the signals of disaster already begun to show?

And if, in the future, there truly exists a form of intelligence more complex than humanity——
looking back at us as we now look back at cyanobacteria——
in its eyes, are we not also missing some crucial field of vision?
Are we not also, driven by selfish instincts and interests,
gestating something of which we have absolutely no concept?
